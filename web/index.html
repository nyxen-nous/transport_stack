How the estimates work

The bus fleet is the sensor. Every DTC bus reports its position every 10–20 seconds. For each bus we find the moments it passes each stop and when it waits there. Two consecutive stops give one measured trip along that stretch of road: about 3.5 million measurements a day.

Why not the timetable? Delhi's published timetable assumes every bus moves at exactly 15 km/h on every road at every hour. Measured speeds range from about 36 km/h at night to about 22 km/h in the evening peak, and vary much more between roads.

Every estimate depends on the hour. Each stretch has its own time for each half hour on weekdays, Saturdays and Sundays/holidays. Where a stretch has little data, it borrows from the same stretch at other hours, then from nearby roads, then the city average. The planner tells you which.

Live mode adjusts the next hour using buses that crossed the same stretches in the last 30 minutes. The adjustment fades as the trip goes further into the future.

The range (e.g. 38–51 min) covers 8 in 10 real bus trips at that time, measured on a held-out week.
